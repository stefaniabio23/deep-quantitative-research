# Interpretation Checklist

Used by the **interpretation critic** to review Phase 4 synthesis and Phase 5 skeptic review.

Focus: whether findings are correctly contextualised, appropriately hedged, and domain-calibrated.
Evaluate against this checklist. Return PASS, REVISE, or FAIL.
Cite the exact checklist item number that failed.

---

## I1: Domain calibration

- [ ] I1.1 — Effect sizes are benchmarked against domain-appropriate standards (not just labelled "large" or "small" in the abstract)
- [ ] I1.2 — Finance: Sharpe ratio, R², factor attribution benchmarks from interpretation-rubric.md are applied
- [ ] I1.3 — Biotech: hazard ratios, ORR, and endpoint significance are compared to SoC and historical benchmarks
- [ ] I1.4 — Quant: factor loadings, PCA variance explained, and correlation structure are interpreted with reference to known benchmarks

## I2: Hedging calibration

- [ ] I2.1 — High-confidence language ("the data shows", "strong evidence") is used only for findings with confidence score 8+
- [ ] I2.2 — Moderate-confidence language ("suggests", "moderate evidence") is used for scores 6-7
- [ ] I2.3 — Low-confidence language is used for scores below 6
- [ ] I2.4 — Findings are not presented as stronger than the weakest link in the evidence chain (data quality + methods + sample)

## I3: What the finding does NOT say

- [ ] I3.1 — Each primary finding includes at least one explicit statement of what it does not establish
- [ ] I3.2 — Causation is not implied where only correlation was measured
- [ ] I3.3 — Generalisability beyond the specific sample, period, and universe is not implied
- [ ] I3.4 — Practical implications are proportionate to the effect size (a small but statistically significant effect is not treated as actionable without further context)

## I4: Skeptic review quality

- [ ] I4.1 — At least one alternative explanation is proposed for each primary finding
- [ ] I4.2 — Challenges are substantiated (not just "correlation is not causation" without a specific alternative)
- [ ] I4.3 — The skeptic does not self-resolve challenges without evidence (a challenge marked "resolved" must reference specific analysis)
- [ ] I4.4 — WEAKENED or OVERTURNED verdicts are reflected in the final confidence score
- [ ] I4.5 — "What would change this verdict" is specific (names a test, dataset, or analysis), not generic
